🧠 Memory Tricks (Mnemonics)

💡 ADME Sequence

"A Dog Meets Elephant"

A = Absorption → D = Distribution → M = Metabolism → E = Excretion

💡 PHARMACOKINETICS vs PHARMACODYNAMICS

"PK = Body on Drug, PD = Drug on Body"

PK: What the Body does to the drug

PD: What the Drug does to the body

💡 PRODRUGS - "ECC"

E = Enalapril → Enalaprilat

C = Codeine → Morphine

C = Clopidogrel → Active metabolite

💡 ZERO-ORDER DRUGS - "EAP"

E = Ethanol (alcohol)

A = Aspirin (high doses)

P = Phenytoin

💡 RECEPTOR TYPES BY SPEED

"Fast Ion, Slow Gene"

I = Ion channel (ms) → G = G-protein (sec) → E = Enzyme (min) → N = Nuclear (hrs)

💡 ION TRAPPING

"Acid trapped in Base, Base trapped in Acid"

Weak acids (aspirin) → Alkalinize urine to trap

Weak bases (cocaine) → Acidify urine to trap

💡 CYP ENZYME INDUCERS

"PCR" (like PCR test)

P = Phenytoin

C = Carbamazepine

R = Rifampin

💡 AGONIST TYPES

"Full, Partial, Inverse" = "Up, Part-way, Down"

Full agonist = 100% efficacy (up)

Partial agonist = <100% efficacy (part-way)

Inverse agonist = <0% efficacy (down, reverses baseline)

💡 METABOLIZER PHENOTYPES

"Poor, Intermediate, Extensive, Ultra" = "PIEU"

P = Poor (PM) - very slow

I = Intermediate (IM) - one functional allele

E = Extensive (EM) - normal

U = Ultra-rapid (UM) - gene duplication

🧠 Active Recall (Click to Reveal)

What is the definition of pharmacokinetics?

Pharmacokinetics: What the body does to the drug - includes ADME (Absorption, Distribution, Metabolism, Excretion)

What is the definition of pharmacodynamics?

Pharmacodynamics: What the drug does to the body - mechanism of action, receptor interactions

What is the first-pass effect?

Drugs are metabolized before reaching systemic circulation (e.g., in liver after oral administration). Nitroglycerin has 90% first-pass clearance, so use sublingual route.

What are the three types of drug transport?

1. Passive diffusion - no energy, no carrier
2. Facilitated diffusion - no energy, needs carrier
3. Active transport - needs energy (ATP) and carrier

What is the key for pH and drug absorption?

Weak acids (aspirin) absorb in acidic environment (stomach).
Weak bases (amphetamine) absorb in basic environment (intestine).

Differentiate first-order vs zero-order kinetics

First-order: Constant fraction eliminated per unit time (most drugs)
Zero-order: Constant amount eliminated per unit time (ethanol, aspirin, phenytoin)

What is the difference between Phase I and Phase II metabolism?

Phase I: Oxidation, reduction, hydrolysis (CYP450) - introduces functional groups
Phase II: Conjugation (glucuronidation, sulfation) - makes drugs more water-soluble for excretion

Give three examples of prodrugs

1. Enalapril → Enalaprilat
2. Codeine → Morphine
3. Clopidogrel → Active metabolite

What is ion trapping for drug elimination?

Weak acids (aspirin): Alkalinize urine with NaHCO₃ (drug becomes ionized, trapped)
Weak bases (cocaine): Acidify urine with NH₄Cl (drug becomes ionized, trapped)

How many half-lives to reach steady state?

4-5 half-lives - this is when rate of administration equals rate of elimination

Formula for Loading Dose?

Loading Dose = Vd × Target Concentration / F
(F = bioavailability, for IV F=1)

What are the four receptor types and their speed?

1. Ligand-gated ion channels - milliseconds (nicotinic ACh)
2. G-protein coupled - seconds (muscarinic)
3. Enzyme-linked - minutes (insulin)
4. Intracellular (nuclear) - hours (steroids)

What is the difference between potency and efficacy?

Potency: Amount needed for effect (EC50) - lower EC50 = more potent
Efficacy: Maximum effect achievable (Emax) - higher Emax = more efficacious

What is the Therapeutic Index formula?

TI = LD₅₀ / ED₅₀
Higher TI = Safer drug (wider margin between therapeutic and toxic doses)

What is an inverse agonist?

Drug that binds to receptor and produces opposite effect of agonist - reduces constitutive/baseline activity below zero (negative efficacy)

Difference between competitive vs noncompetitive antagonist?

Competitive: Reversible, surmountable by more agonist, rightward shift of curve
Noncompetitive: Irreversible or allosteric, insurmountable, reduces Emax

Difference between mutation and polymorphism?

Mutation: < 1% population, rare diseases
Polymorphism: > 1% population, SNPs account for 90% of genetic variation

What are the metabolizer phenotypes?

EM (Extensive) - normal, two functional alleles
PM (Poor) - very slow, two nonfunctional alleles
IM (Intermediate) - one functional, one nonfunctional
UM (Ultra-Rapid) - very fast, gene duplication

Clinical significance of CYP2D6 polymorphism

Codeine is converted to morphine by CYP2D6.
UM: Excessive morphine → respiratory depression risk
PM: No analgesia

Clinical significance of CYP2C19 polymorphism

Clopidogrel activation requires CYP2C19.
PM: Reduced activation → use alternative (prasugrel, ticagrelor)
UM: Increased bleeding risk
